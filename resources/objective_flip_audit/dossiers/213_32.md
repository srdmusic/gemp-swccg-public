## Source law

- Route A: after any character is hit by a card owned by the objective owner,
  flip to the back. The source has no `canBeFlipped` guard on this event route.
- Route B: during the owner's battle phase, flip when the owner controls two
  battlegrounds with a gangster at each. Undercover spies cannot contribute to
  control. Cards excluded from battle still count.
- The back flips front unconditionally at the end of every turn. It is a
  temporary payoff window, not a stable side with a hold condition.
- When the front becomes active, the opponent loses exactly 1 Force if the
  owner occupies three battlegrounds. This normally fires on the end-turn
  back-to-front flip, not on the initial front-to-back flip.
- Both sides may once per turn deploy a legal non-unique blaster or a card with
  First Light in its title from Reserve Deck.

  battlegrounds, in the owner's battle phase. It deliberately contains no

- The exact objective pull receives route ownership when Reserve Deck contains
  a currently legal non-unique blaster or First Light card. This broad parent
  keeps Route A alive. Only a First Light battleground receives Route B
  location-progress credit.
- A blaster with no legal warrior host does not receive objective-route credit.
  Pull exhaustion and unrelated sources remain neutral.

  battlegrounds. The final direct deploy receives
  `DEPLOY.OBJECTIVE.SHADOW_COLLECTIVE.ROUTE_COMPLETION +8000` and releases its

  preserves the exact initiation fee only for a legal, formation-safe battle
  accepted by the live battle predictor. A direct completing deploy releases
  that battle reserve.
- Movement prefers a gangster move that creates the second distinct qualified
  battleground. Force-loss selection preserves a still-needed gangster while
  an expendable card exists.
- Route A uses the existing generic legal weapon-fire and target logic. No
  objective-specific target bonus was added. A full public-bot sequence pulls
